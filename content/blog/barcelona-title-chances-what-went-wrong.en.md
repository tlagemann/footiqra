On 19 October, Barcelona looked untouchable. Nine La Liga games, nine wins, a five-point lead and the number one spot in the Footiqra power ranking. Our simulations gave them a **76% chance** of winning the title. Two weeks later, that chance is down to **52%**, and Real Madrid have more than doubled theirs.

So what went wrong? Less than the drop suggests, but one bad week was enough to turn a procession into a race.

## Tuesday: a 4-2 defeat in Paris

The slide started in the Champions League. On 20 October, Barcelona lost 4-2 at Paris Saint-Germain. Our model gave them a 35% chance of winning that game, so a defeat was no shock. But a heavy loss against one of Europe's strongest sides still cost rating points, and Barça dropped from first to second in the power ranking.

The table did not change. What changed was how strong the model thinks Barcelona really are.

## Sunday: losing El Clásico at home

Five days later, on 25 October, came the result that really mattered. Barcelona were 54% favourites at home against Real Madrid and lost 1-2.

That one game did double damage. The lead over Real shrank from six points to three, and Real's rating climbed while Barça's fell. When the chances are recalculated with a smaller lead **and** a closer gap in strength, the simulations produce a very different picture. Across those six days, Barcelona's title chance fell by 22 percentage points.

## The quiet part: Real Madrid got much better

The bigger story is not Barcelona's dip but Real Madrid's rise. Real have not lost since 20 September. In October and early November they won all six matches, including 4-3 at Roma, 4-1 against Sevilla and RB Leipzig, 2-1 at the Camp Nou and 3-0 at Racing Santander.

On 19 October, Barcelona were rated 64 points stronger than Real Madrid. Today the gap is just 13 points. Real are now fourth in Europe, right behind Barça in third. Two clubs that close in strength, separated by three points after eleven games, make a genuine title race.

## So did anything really go wrong?

Not much, if you look at the whole season:

- **Barça still lead La Liga** with 30 points from 11 games, ahead of Atlético (28) and Real Madrid (27).
- **They bounced back immediately**, beating Alavés 6-1.
- **They are still the third-best team in Europe** in the power ranking, behind only Arsenal and PSG.

What went wrong was timing: two defeats in one week, one of them against the direct rival, at the exact moment Real Madrid hit form. A 76% chance was never a guarantee. It meant Barça would lose the title in roughly one season out of four, and the last two weeks showed how quickly that can happen.

## What comes next

The next test arrives immediately: **Atlético Madrid vs Barcelona on 8 November**. Atlético are only two points behind, so a defeat would cost Barça the top spot. The return Clásico at the Bernabéu follows on 9 May.
